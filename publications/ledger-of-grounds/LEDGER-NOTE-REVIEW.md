# Adversarial review of the ledger-of-grounds note

Three lenses — prior art, code-vs-text, legal accuracy — then a skeptic per
finding told to refute it. **30 raised, 24 survived.**

Four were re-verified by hand and all four hold:

* the note says 115 stands; `run_all.py` lists **117**;
* the Lean check in `run_all.py` is a substring test over combined output,
  satisfied by **one** conforming object out of **217** that print axioms;
* the bracket of §2 is `len(fallout)` — a COUNT OF CLAIMS — while §1 argues
  at length that counting claims is the wrong question and exposure must be
  by unit. The two headline metrics contradict each other;
* Debian's `|` is an ordered preference list over interchangeable providers
  (`libcurl3-gnutls | libcurl3-nss | libcurl4`), not a declaration of
  independence. §3's convergence argument reads the outside world wrongly.

## THE COMPLETENESS CRITIC

More valuable than the lenses. Reproduced in full:

**Structural miss.** All three lenses are *external-reference* lenses: each holds a sentence against an authority outside the note (the literature, the program, the law). None can catch (a) claims with no external referent — intent, priority, effort; (b) incoherence *between* two sections that are each individually accurate; (c) credibility transferred from a verified part to an unverified one; (d) the note's own apparatus (§8, the reproduction claims); (e) **absences** — no lens can flag "there is no evaluation", because a missing thing has no sentence to point at.

Concrete unexamined claims:

**1. The Lean warrant (Abstract, §5 bullet 5, §8) — nobody opened `lean/`.** It is the note's only rigor claim and no finding touches it. `/media/vitaly/SSD_1000GB/Projects/ZTL/lean/` is 28 modules with 217 `#print axioms`, and greps for `retract`, `fallout`, `bracket`, independence-of-grounds return zero: the machine-checked object is a three-valued calculus (Knaster–Tarski, tableaux, quantifiers), and not one of the note's three advertised properties is a theorem there. §8's half-concession ("concerns the calculus, not the ledger") does not cover the abstract's "a few hundred lines **over** a calculus machine-checked on an empty axiom list", which asserts a derivation relation nobody checked. Worse, the check §8 tells the reader to run is the weaker of the two in the repo: `run_all.py:517-518` sets `lean_ok` from `"does not depend on any axioms" in r.stdout + r.stderr` — a substring test satisfied by **one** conforming object out of 217. The corpus's actual per-object audit, `inventory/axiom_audit.py`, is not mentioned in §8.

**2. §8 itself got no lens.** "115 stands" — `run_all.py`'s `STANDS` list has 117 entries. "Standard library only, no dependencies" sits three lines above a command that shells out to `lake build` under `leanprover/lean4:v4.29.1`; and `db/probe_real.py` reads `/var/lib/dpkg/status`, so §3 reproduces only on a dpkg machine. "Every figure is printed by a program a reader can run" was checked for *fidelity* and never for *runnability*.

**3. §1 vs §2 — the two headline metrics are not the same metric.** §2's bracket is `len(fallout)` (`zbook.py:582`): a count of claims. §1's boast is that counting claims is the wrong question and exposure must be reported **by unit**, never summed across incommensurables. `exposure()`'s own docstring (`zbook.py:589-596`) says so outright — counting claims "was answering a different question from the one an auditor asks first". So the metric that carries the width has no units, and the metric with units has no width; a claim count adds a fee-claim to an area-claim into one integer, which is the addition §1 says the machine refuses. The code lens verified printed figures against the program; no one asked whether §1 and §2 are about the same object.

**4. §3 — the analogy's premise, not its arithmetic.** The counts were re-derived; the semantics were not. Debian's `Depends: libfoo | libbar` is an **ordered preference list** ("first available satisfies", routinely resolved through virtual packages/`Provides`) and declares nothing whatever about independence. The note's own `|` is defined nine lines earlier as "declares that two grounds are **independent**". "The same notation for the same idea" is true of the notation and false of the idea, so the load-bearing inference — two fields converged and "stopped at the same wall" — collapses: dpkg never walked toward that wall. Also "a package manager forty years old": dpkg is 1993-94.

**5. §6, the Wirecard paragraph.** "**Measured** against the Wirecard collapse" implies data. The corpus's Wirecard artefact is a small synthetic book built 2026-08-16 (`zbook.py:1246`, `zbook.py:645`); there is no Wirecard input. Neither the case facts (€1.9bn, "forged bank letters") nor the counterfactual ("would have printed a clean EARNED for years") was checked by anyone — the legal lens read doctrine, not the note's one empirical illustration.

**6. Nobody asked whether the artifact has ever been used.** No user, no case, no baseline, no comparison run, no encoding of a real matter. Every example is `inv-17` / `order-4` / `photo-a`. The closing sentence — worth having "depends on whether the refusals are the ones a particular reader needs" — has never been put to such a reader. A fourth lens ("does it work on anything real, and against what alternative?") would find the note's evidence base is a toy book authored by the claimant.

**7. The threat model is undeclared and unexamined.** §2 sells the width as "the price of the author's unverifiable word"; §6 concedes the machine is **inert** against anyone controlling input. The declarations, the book and the names are all authored by the same party, so the bracket prices honest error only and is worth exactly zero against the adversary §6 names. No lens here is a trust-model lens.

**8. Unfalsifiable claims doing real argumentative work.** "The notation was arrived at here independently, from a philosophical problem about regress" (§3) — a priority claim with no external referent, and it is the premise of the whole convergence argument. Likewise "the omission is a design decision rather than an oversight" (§2), "could be added in an afternoon" (§5), "the line is drawn rather than blurred" (§3). These are claims about intent and history that no prior-art, code, or legal check can reach — and each is placed where it converts a limitation into a virtue.

**9. The disclosure architecture as rhetoric.** §§6-7 are ~40% of the length and their function is to buy credence for the single novelty bullet in §5. Three lenses asked whether individual sentences are true; none asked whether candour is being spent as currency — which is the question a hostile reader of a DOI-deposited note will ask first.

### 23. [MAJOR] §5, bullet 3 — support vs permission
*lens: prior-art*

**Quote.** **It keeps support and permission apart in the same store.** Authorization logics separate them for access control [3, 4, 5]; ledgers of claims do not separate them at all.

**Defect.** Wrong on both halves. (a) [3] and [4] do not separate evidential support from permission: the ABLP calculus has no evidential dimension at all — `says` and `speaks for` are wholly within the authority dimension, distinguishing which principal's assertion counts, not evidence from entitlement. Citing them for a separation they do not make weakens rather than supports the sentence. (b) 'Ledgers of claims do not separate them at all' is false of the argumentation and legal-reasoning literature the note is aimed at. Toulmin's *The Uses of Argument* (Cambridge UP, 1958) separates **data** (what supports) from **warrant** (what licenses the step) and **backing** (what entitles the warrant), with **qualifier** and **rebuttal** as first-class elements — and this corpus uses Toulmin's own word 'warrant' on nearly every page while Toulmin appears nowhere in the reference list. For the note's declared audience the separation is also the oldest thing in the room: the legal syllogism's major premise (norm) and minor premise (fact), Hohfeld's analysis of jural relations (1913/1917), and evidence law's split between admissibility and probative weight. Deontic-versus-epistemic modality has been a formal separation since von Wright (1951).

**Attack.** 'A corporate lawyer will read "a fact survives the repeal of a statute, and a statute survives the discrediting of a witness" and recognise the major and minor premise of a syllogism, which is not thirty years old but two thousand. ### 24. [MINOR] §1, the incommensurability paragraph
*lens: law*

**Quote.** This last is worth a sentence for a reader from law rather than engineering. The machine's answer to an ill-posed comparison is not an error and not a guess: it is a fourth verdict, computed, meaning *these two cannot be compared and here is why*.

**Defect.** This is the one paragraph explicitly addressed to a legal reader, and it gets the legal posture backwards. Commensuration of the incommensurable is what legal systems are built to do and what they are judged on: damages convert pain, lost years and reputational harm into money; proportionality and balancing tests weigh incommensurable interests as a matter of doctrine; contribution and apportionment divide a single sum across parties whose fault has no common unit; insolvency ranks claims of different natures in one queue. Refusing to add metres to roubles is correct arithmetic and a poor advertisement to a lawyer: the note offers as a feature the refusal of the operation a remedies or a public-law practice performs daily. What the note could honestly claim — that the refusal makes the *point of commensuration* explicit and attributable to a human rather than hiding it in a total — is the interesting claim and is not made.

**Attack.** "You singled me out for this paragraph. My job is to turn a dead man's earning years into a number and to weigh a privacy interest against a public one. Your machine's proudest verdict is that it declines to do either. If your point is that the conversion should be a named human act rather than a silent addition, say that; as written you have told a lawyer that your tool refuses the central operation of his craft and expected him to be pleased."

**Correction.** Replace the paragraph at /media/vitaly/SSD_1000GB/Projects/ZTL/paper/ledger-of-grounds-note.md lines 73-76 with something on these lines: "This last is worth a sentence for a reader from law rather than engineering — who commensurates the incommensurable for a living, turning lost years into money and weighing a privacy interest against a public one.
