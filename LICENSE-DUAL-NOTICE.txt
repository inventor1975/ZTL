WHY TWO. The MIT licence is short and permissive but says nothing about
patents. Apache 2.0 permits the same things and adds an explicit patent grant
from every contributor, plus a termination clause for anyone who sues over
patents in this code. For an individual the difference is small. For an
organisation it is the clause its counsel looks for first, and its absence is
what turns a decision into an escalation.

Adding Apache 2.0 rather than replacing MIT is deliberate: every earlier
publication of this work states that the code is MIT-licensed, and those
statements remain true. Nothing is withdrawn from anyone; an option is added.

Dual licensing on these two terms follows the practice of the Rust project.
