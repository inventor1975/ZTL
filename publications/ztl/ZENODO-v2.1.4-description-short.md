ZTL (Zero-Trust Logic) is a propositional logic for dirty data: parameters owned by another system, which may change them between two reads, so that each occurrence of a dirty atom is read on its own. Atoms take three values, T, F and Z (dirty). Each connective is the lift of a classical one: it returns T only if T is forced under every classical reading of its dirty arguments, and F otherwise. No compound takes Z. The guarantee is per connective. A negation applied to a compound's F can yield T where not every reading supports it, as ¬¬p with p dirty shows.

Proved in Lean, on the empty axiom list:
- On clean data ZTL is classical logic (evalF_agrees), and every ZTL validity is a classical tautology (ztl_taut_is_classical).
- No compound takes Z (evalF_classical).
- ¬¬p ⊨ p fails in ZTL and holds in every matrix whose negation is involutive (signature).
- Where no dirty atom stands under a negation, a T survives every cleaning (no_gift).

Measured, on stated finite pools:
- In the census of twenty-six classical laws, twelve hold on a dirty atom and fourteen are refuted with an exhibited witness.
- ZTL differs from Bochvar's external connectives in seven cells or in eight, depending on the convention for external negation.
- Over all formulas of depth ≤ 2 over three atoms, every F of ZTL is witnessed by a reading of the dirty occurrences on which the formula is false.

The repository's judge reports, beside each verdict, whether it survives every cleaning; a T that does not is reported as ON CREDIT.

The preprint also covers a signed tableau calculus, quantifiers, a temporal layer, identity and free logic, and formalised connections to six engineering traditions. Its contents and the notes of every earlier version are in the PDF.
