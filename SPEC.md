# ZTL — Zero-Trust Logic

Specification v0.2 — 2026-07-11. Status: word-first phase closed, the
test bench works, the passport (preprint §10) is adopted: three values T, F, Z; every
connective two-valued; Z the value of atoms only.

## The principle (one, generates everything)

**Truth is never granted on credit.** When the mark Z ("truth not
earned") on an input reaches any connective, it is read in all classical
ways at once, and the connective returns T only if T is forced under
**every** reading; otherwise F. Every occurrence of Z is substituted
independently; the result is always classical — Z evaporates at the
first touch of an operator (greedy collapse).

Formally: `op(...Z...) = ⋀ { op(...v...) : v ∈ {T,F} }` over each
Z-argument.

## Values and marks

Values: `T` (earned), `F` (falsehood), `Z` — zero-trust, "not earned", the
mark of a dirty atom — not ours: its owner may change it between two reads, so each occurrence is read on its own; verification cleans it, replaces it with a checked value (a property of an atomic datum; in the design
dialogues of 2026-07-10 it appeared as "L"). ZTL is three-valued and every
connective is two-valued: the verdict of every compound is T or F, and Z is
the value of atoms only (a claim that is one dirty atom answers Z).
Until 2026-09-28 this said "Z is not a truth value" — too strong: the atom
is an assertion, and its value is Z. The solver's service state `N` ("not yet
computed", present only under self-reference, never escapes outward) is
neither a value nor a mark. The lazy register reuses the symbol Z
positionally for this phase (the role differs, not the alphabet —
preprint §10: Kleene's third element conflates the mark and the phase
by making their shared symbol a value of assertions).

**The boundary register (STAKED, NOT BUILT — IDEAS 12.6).** The four
letters above are the letters of the LOGIC and stay four. Outside them,
at the seam with the world, a second register is staked and deliberately
not implemented: two marks and two states, mirroring the internal ladder
by how much an event carries.

**Two orderings, and the axis must be named.** The rungs below are
ordered by how much an event CARRIES. That is not the only order: the
GENETIC one — what was recognised first — runs `N → F → T → Z` inside
and `E → M → σ → O` at the seam. Falsity precedes truth (denial is
cheap, assertion is earned), and the mark comes LAST, because "an input
may be unverified, and that is not a third truth value" is a modern
recognition: IEEE NaN 1985, SQL NULL 1986. The pairing is unaffected —
a pairing is not an ordering. (IDEAS 12.7.)

The table reads ONE WAY — from the physical INTO the operational. It is
not a mirror of two alphabets but a map of ARRIVAL: a boundary letter
arrives and becomes an internal one. The logic's alphabet is what the
seam delivered.

| at the seam (source) | → | inside (arrival) | why the pair |
|---|---|---|---|
| `E` — the world stayed silent | → | `N` phase | `E` **is** the premature read of phase `N` (§10) — same subject |
| `M` (matter) — no ground was ever offered | → | `F` — not earned | empty to empty: nothing offered, therefore nothing earned |
| `σ` — the world ANSWERED | → | `T` — earned | the world's answer is what an earned value is paid in |
| `O` — the world's INDETERMINACY (ontic: there is no value) | → | `Z` mark (epistemic: a value exists, we have not looked) | one rung, differing in WHY the value is absent |

A pair goes by what a letter is ABOUT, not by what it causes. The
world's silence does produce `F` — `¬Z = F` is literally "asked,
nothing came back, therefore not earned" — but that is a consequence,
while `E`'s subject is the phase it read too early.

So `E` carries no logical content of its own, which explains rather than
overturns the preprint's §10 line — *"the system has no error letter,
and none is missing… E is hardware, not logic"*. It has no letter inside
because it is an interface accident about a phase that has no value yet,
not a value of any kind. The other three do carry content: `M` is an
input that never came, `O` is ontic vacancy as opposed to epistemic `Z`,
and `σ` is an answer that can disagree with what was derived.

`M` and `O` are MARKS, `E` and `σ` are STATES: a boundary mark lives on
an atom and never becomes a boundary event, exactly as `Z` never becomes
a verdict. Nothing in this repository implements `M` or `O`; `M` may
already be working unnamed (`vrg/epoch_artifact.py` rejects a verify
with no `source_reference` — "ground never offered", distinct from `Z`),
while `O` has no live user yet and so is not minted.

## Tables (generated by the principle; MEASURED ztl.py)

```
 ¬ : T→F, F→T, Z→F
 ∧ : the Z-row and Z-column are all F
 ∨ : Z∨T=T, otherwise every cell with Z is F
 → : Z→T=T, F→Z=T, every other cell with Z is F   (T→Z=F, Z→Z=F, Z→F=F)
 ⊕ : every cell with Z is F
 ↔ : every cell with Z is F
```

Anchor axiom cells (dictated at design time, reproduced by the
principle, checked by `tests_axioms()`): ¬Z=F, ¬¬Z=T, Z↔Z=F, Z↔T=F,
Z⊕Z=F, Z⊕F=F, Z⊕T=F.

## Reading the text: precedence (MEASURED `ztljudge.formalize`, 2026-09-28)

Binding, strongest first: `~` (prefix), then `&` > `|` > `^` > `->` > `=` (also `<->`).
Every binary connective is LEFT-associative. So `a & b | c` is `(a & b) | c`,
`a | b ^ c` is `(a | b) ^ c`, `a = b -> c` is `a = (b -> c)`, and — the one that differs
from the usual convention — `a -> b -> c` is `(a -> b) -> c`, not `a -> (b -> c)`.
Write the brackets when you mean the other reading. The grammar is consistent: every text
parses to one tree or is refused, and printing a tree then re-reading it is the identity
(11,111,111 token strings, red team PR #5). Before 2026-09-28 this was stated nowhere.

## Fixed forks (decided; do not reopen without cause)

1. **¬Z = F, not "Z collapses to F at the atom".** Theorem: {¬Z=F,
   T→Z=F} are incompatible with contraposition. Contraposition was
   sacrificed deliberately; atomic collapse would turn ZTL verbatim
   into Bochvar's isomorph B3□ and erase all novelty.
2. **Greedy collapse, not lazy flow** (SQL NULL is the lazy relative:
   Z flows, falsehood once at the WHERE boundary). Greediness = the
   Tarski ladder in evaluation order: every operator over Z is a floor;
   ¬¬Z=T is a report about a report, not "laundering of truth".
3. **Z↔Z = F — quarantine is detectable**: isZ(x) = ¬(x↔x) is
   expressible inside the language. The price: the avenger liar μ is
   expressible too — "μ is false or isZ(μ)"; its content evaluates to
   T while μ is denied truth. The bill is paid deliberately.
4. **Paradoxes are extinguished by the quarantine flag, not by the
   tables**: ¬ has no fixed point, the liar cannot be seated by the
   tables. Z-sentences are exempted from the Tarski schema (the demand
   v(sentence)=v(content) is not levied on them).

## Which laws extend to a marked atom (MEASURED audit.py; re-measured 2026-09-18)

**All 26 hold on verified data — not one falls.** On mark-free valuations ZTL and
classical logic validate the same 588 formulas of the depth-≤2 pool, the same set
element for element. The split below is a different question: which of the 26 keep
working when an atom carries the mark. The 14 that do not are not lost — on a marked
atom ZTL proves the refutation there instead, with the witness cell named.

**Extend to the mark (12):** modus ponens (semantic), non-contradiction
¬(p∧¬p), transitivity of →, commutativity of ∧ and ∨, associativity of
∧ and ∨, both distributivities, and the three canonical definitions:
p→q = ¬p∨q, p⊕q = (p∧¬q)∨(¬p∧q), p↔q = (p∧q)∨(¬p∧¬q) = (p→q)∧(q→p).

**Fallen laws (14) — a manifesto, not an apology:** double negation,
both De Morgan laws, contraposition, ⊕=¬(↔), idempotence of ∧ and ∨,
absorption, the units (p∧T=p, p∨F=p), excluded middle, reflexivity
p→p, Peirce's law, affirmation of the consequent q→(p→q).
Common trait of the fallen: all break only on Z, and all are laws that
grant truth "for free" (via polarity flip or tautologousness of form).

**Greediness theorem (MEASURED):** no compound formula ever takes the
value Z; the whole system is generated from {¬,∧,∨} (→, ⊕, ↔ are
definable by the surviving identities above).

Important: **modus ponens is ALIVE** (T→Z=F blocks the counterexample) —
ZTL is stronger in inference than Priest's LP, where MP falls.

## Place on the map (literature; checked 2026-07-10 against
Karpenko–Tomova 2017)

* Home: **Bochvar's B3 (1938)**, the external fragment. ¬, ∧, ∨
  coincide cell-for-cell with his ⌉, ∩□, ∪□. All ZTL connectives are
  external functions ⇒ functionally ZTL embeds in B3; there are no new
  functions (there never are — Finn).
* The delta: **7 cells** in →, ↔, ⊕ (Z→Z, Z→F, Z↔Z, Z↔F, F↔Z, Z⊕T,
  T⊕Z) — a systematic closing of the places where Bochvar's quarantine
  grants vacuous truth. ZTL's implication = his ◇A ⊃ □B, taken as a
  primitive.
* ZTL is **outside Tomova's class of "natural implications"**, and
  precisely: of her four criteria it MEETS (1) C-extension (proved for
  the whole language, `lean/ClassicalAgreement.lean`) and (2)
  Łukasiewicz–Tarski normality (modus ponens preserves the designated
  value), and VIOLATES (3) "p ≤ q ⇒ p→q designated" in exactly ONE cell,
  (Z, Z) — MEASURED. p→p is not a primitive of the definition; it is the
  diagonal of (3), and (3) presupposes the middle value is a DEGREE of
  truth on a linear order. Z is a status mark barred from compounds, so
  the condition has no subject here. The constitutive test is met
  elsewhere: the consequence relation is Tarskian, p ⊨ p holds where
  ⊨ p→p fails. Expect this as the first objection from logicians, and
  answer with the one cell, not with a shrug.
* Kindred in spirit: supervaluationism (rigid, non-tabular), SQL NULL
  (lazy), IEEE NaN (comparisons fail), exceptions with try/catch at
  every node (greediness). The motivation "greedy local supervaluation
  / default deny" was not found in the literature — not found ≠ does
  not exist.

## The keel: the entailment relation (MEASURED entailment.py,
2026-07-10)

Γ ⊨ φ ⟺ every valuation making all premises T makes the conclusion T
(only {T} is designated). Tarskian by construction: reflexivity,
monotonicity, cut follow from the form of the definition.

**The main discovery of this stage — the rules-versus-laws split:**
classical logic cannot tell them apart (the deduction theorem glues
them), ZTL separates them sharply. Of 14 classical inference rules,
**12** survive, including contraposition-as-a-rule (p→q ⊨ ¬q→¬p — even
though the identity law fell!), the K-rule (q ⊨ p→q), explosion and
resolution. Two fall: ¬¬-elimination (¬¬p ⊨ p, counterexample p=Z — Z
leaks through double negation) and "tautology in the conclusion"
(p ⊨ q∨¬q — a fresh atom in the conclusion earns no truth).

**The deduction theorem — left-to-right only**: ⊨A→B implies A⊨B (that
is MP), but A⊨B does NOT imply ⊨A→B (p⊨p, yet ⊭p→p). The arrow is
stricter than entailment — it does not internalize ⊨; it is a verdict
that must be earned. Consequence for the calculus stage: build with
sequents (rules), not axioms (laws).

**Terminological honesty:** ZTL is **paracomplete** (gaps exist, LEM
fell), but NOT paraconsistent in the ⊨ sense: v(p)=v(¬p)=T is
unattainable, there are no gluts, explosion is vacuously valid. The
liar is extinguished by quarantine (non-assertion), not by tolerance of
contradiction.

## Lean's three axioms, read in ZTL (2026-11-06)

The Lean corpus uses none of Lean's three standard axioms; the reason is operational (the curator's principle), and
ZTL gives it content. Each axiom says "this need not be checked": propext ("what is equivalent need not be told
apart"), Quot.sound and the funext it yields ("what agrees on every observation is the same"), Classical.choice and
the excluded middle it yields ("A or not A is decided"). Read with ZTL's values, each fails exactly on a dirty input
and holds on clean ones — `lean/ZAxiomsRead.lean`, zero axioms:

* propext: p and p ∧ p are interderivable, yet ¬(p ∧ p) is T and ¬p is not, at p = Z; without "clean", "T exactly
  together" is not equality (F and Z); on clean values it is, so they are interchangeable in every context.
* Quot.sound / funext by observation: x and x ∧ x agree on T and on F and differ at Z; for ONE connective, agreeing on
  the clean inputs is agreeing everywhere. The boundary is per connective, not per formula, as for degrees.
* Excluded middle: A ∨ ¬A is not T at A = Z, and is T on every clean A.
* The axiom of choice (`lean/ZChoiceRead.lean`, finite lists). Proved: what holds for every element holds of a pick
  under any reading of its uses; "two uses denote the same element" holds at every stable reading and fails at some
  independent one (two elements); "the pick is this element" holds at one stable reading and fails at another; a
  choice function on finitely many non-empty lists is built, with no axiom. Reading, not proved: a pick no one can
  exhibit is an unchecked value used stably, so the axiom, whose content is infinite families, is the judge's IF
  STABLE stipulated for infinitely many picks at once.
* Its ancestor, Hilbert's ε (introduced in the early 1920s; the epsilon theorems in Hilbert–Bernays 1939).
  Proved (`lean/ZEpsilon.lean`, a non-empty finite list, a computable property): with picks constrained by the
  critical axiom, "P(ε)" holds for every admissible pick exactly when a witness exists, found by search with no
  excluded middle (for non-computable P the critical axiom brings the excluded middle); which element ε is stays
  undetermined; two admissible picks can differ. Reading: a finite, semantic counterpart for P(ε) of the epsilon
  theorems, which are about proofs; ZTL's addition is to make explicit, and judge, the two readings of an unchecked
  value — read anew (dirty) or the same everywhere (stable).

So the analogues are usable where no dirty parameter is involved. This does NOT make the axioms false in Lean: they
are consistent there and hold in the usual set-theoretic model. It makes them stipulations whose ZTL readings break
on unchecked data — the reason to keep them out by default; a proof on the empty list stays valid whoever adds them.

## Degrees (draft for ZTL 2.2, 2026-11-05)

The same ZTL, read at a finer grain; optional. A statement may carry a
**degree** — read here as the probability that it is true (a modelling
choice), a point or an interval inside [0, 1]. The three values are three
degrees: T = [1, 1], F = [0, 0], Z = the whole [0, 1] — not a middle
degree but no constraint at all, which is why Tomova's criterion (3)
above, which needs a middle degree on a linear order, has no subject.
How two statements are joined is **not known**, so a compound's degree is
the interval over every joint distribution that keeps each part's own
degree (the Fréchet bounds; for two parts P(a ∧ b) ∈
[max(0, a+b−1), min(a, b)]). This is the classical probability-logic
construction of Boole (1854), Fréchet (1935), Hailperin and Nilsson
(1986); its ends are the lower and upper probabilities of this credal
set in Walley's sense.

**Two maps, not one — keep them apart:**

* **A connective's answer** (output): T when the lower end of its degree
  is 1 — every admissible joint puts all its weight where the connective
  holds — and F otherwise. F here means "not earned" (the principle at
  the top: T only if forced, otherwise F), not "degree 0":
  ¬a at a = 0.3 has degree 0.7 and the connective answers F.
* **A degree as an input value**: [1, 1] → T, [0, 0] → F, anything else →
  Z, the smallest of the three degrees that contains it. Lossy by design:
  the judge works on T, F, Z, and the degree itself is kept beside it.

**What is proved and what is measured:**

* **Each table is the output map of its degrees.** For EVERY one- and
  two-argument connective and every input among T, F, Z: PROVED, zero
  axioms (`lean/ZDegrees.lean`: `projection`, `projection1`). Checked
  against the PUBLISHED tables too — the site's 4 one-argument and 16
  two-argument tables (4·3 + 16·9 = 156 cells), the curator's file
  (2 + 16), the code's six connectives: 0 cells differ
  (`zfrechet_tables.py`; flipping any one of the 156 site cells is
  caught).
* **This holds per connective, not per formula.** ZTL reads a compound
  node by node, each connective reading the T or F below it. ¬(a ∧ b) at
  Z, Z: the inner ∧ answers F, ¬F answers T — yet a joint keeping both
  degrees gives ¬(a ∧ b) degree 0
  (`ZDegrees.compound_is_node_by_node`). The two readings answer
  different questions: the node-by-node value is what each connective
  answers about its own inputs (the greedy register); the judge's
  disposition answers whether the whole claim is forced. MEASURED: on
  400 random formulas over 3 atoms × all 27 markings (10 800 judgments,
  `zfrechet.py`) the judge's EARNED is exactly "lower end of the whole
  formula's degree = 1", REFUTED exactly "upper end = 0", and EARNED /
  REFUTED IF STABLE the same with a dirty atom's occurrences read as one.
  The judge is computed separately (`ztljudge`, all classical
  readings), but the agreement is EXPECTED, not a discovery: the extreme
  joints of the Fréchet set are exactly the classical readings, so
  "lower end 1" and "true in every reading" coincide. The measurement
  checks the two implementations against each other; it is not a
  theorem for larger formulas.
* **It extends §16.** E9 proved an atom's verdict to be the
  Dempster–Shafer threshold (T ⟺ Bel = 1, F ⟺ Pl = 0; `ZDempster.lean`,
  zero axioms) — Bel and Pl being the lower and upper probability of the
  credal set. That is the INPUT map; the new step is the connectives.

**Working rules:**

* **The dirty rule is not independence.** A Z atom read at two places is
  two events whose joint is unknown (any joint is allowed) — its owner
  may change it between the two reads — not two independent events;
  `stable=True` reads them as one (the judge's "IF STABLE").
* **Independence is a stipulation**, never a default. Declared, it fixes
  the joint: a point when the degrees are points, an interval otherwise
  (computed exactly at the corners, the function being multilinear).
  It may be declared between GROUPS (2026-11-06): `independent:
  [["a", "b"], ["c"]]` — the groups independent of one another, the joint
  inside a group still unknown; every statement of the claim in exactly
  one group (an atom in none is refused, not guessed). Exact
  (`zdegree.degree_groups`): the probability is multilinear in the
  groups' distributions, so its ends sit at products of vertices of the
  groups' polytopes — enumerated for the smaller groups (at most 3
  events each), an LP for the largest. Example: a, b, c at 1/2, a ∧ b ∧ c
  is [0, 1/2] with the joint unknown, 1/8 with all independent, and
  [0, 1/4] with {a, b} | {c}. The case that asked for it: a swarm whose
  seat lottery is independent of the liars by construction, while the
  liars' own joint is unknown (`zswarm_bounds.py`).
* **Exact degrees** — `zdegree.py`: two linear programs over the worlds
  of the formula in exact fractions (Bland's rule, no third-party
  solver); each end is certified twice on every computation — a joint
  that attains it and a dual that bounds it, both checked over all
  worlds (a run-time certificate, not a Lean proof). Above 10 events
  (2^10 worlds) it refuses, with the reason. The cheap
  "Fréchet at the corners" is exact on the tables' inputs 0, 1, [0, 1]
  and WRONG on interval inputs in general: for exclusive or, x ∈ [0, 1],
  y = 1/2, the corners give [1/2, 1/2] while the exact interval is
  [0, 1] (computed). The language therefore uses the LP.
* **A threshold is declared, never built in.** In ZFL a row may carry
  `degree`; the document may carry `need` (the threshold) and
  `independent`. The degree section then answers EARNED (lower end ≥
  need), REFUTED (upper end < need), OPEN (between); an EARNED or REFUTED
  becomes ON CREDIT when it rests on a degree that was told rather than
  checked (widening that degree to [0, 1] would move the interval).
  These verdicts are relative to the declared `need` and are not the
  judge's: at need = 1, EARNED here is the judge's EARNED, while REFUTED
  here (upper end below need) is weaker than the judge's (upper end 0). Without `need` it reports the interval only:
  there is no default 0.5.
* **Chance is not a weak link.** A row whose degree is strictly inside
  (0, 1) enters the judge as Z (the input map). Its degree may be
  documented; its truth is settled by no document, only by the observed
  outcome — after which it is a verified or refuted row without a
  degree. Such a row is Z in a second sense — chance, not an unverified
  input — and the report keeps the two apart: the judge names such rows
  as chance, and "what to check" never offers them.

**Where degrees do not apply.** The Fréchet set ranges over joint
distributions, so it presumes that one EXISTS. For statements about
incompatible quantum observables it does not (Bell, Hardy,
Kochen–Specker): the corpus marks an unasked, non-commuting answer
Z_PERMANENT (`dilemmas/hardy.py`, `wigner_friends.py`), and a degree
computed over it would be an answer about a distribution that is not
there. Likewise a probability got by counting what no act witnesses
(branches, `many_worlds.py`) is not a degree. Degrees are for
statements that share one sample space.
